Launch playbook

How a new channel earns its place.

The same four gates for WhatsApp, WeChat and whatever comes next. A channel only moves forward when it clears the exit criteria.

01

Evaluate

  • Where do our buyers actually reply?
  • Provider and business account options.
  • Legal and data protection review.

ExitA market, a use case and a provider, signed off by Legal.

02

Foundations

  • Opt-in and preference capture with Marketing Ops.
  • Data model, CRM sync, lead routing.
  • Approved templates and a tone guide.

ExitConsent is recorded, replies reach the right owner.

03

Pilot

  • One or two use cases, one market.
  • Holdout group from day one.
  • Weekly read with Sales and regional team.

ExitIncremental lift on an agreed funnel metric, low opt-outs.

04

Scale

  • More use cases, then more markets.
  • Playbooks so regions can run it.
  • AI agents on the repeatable parts.

ExitRuns reliably without me in every send.

AI agents

Agents do the volume. People own the judgement.

Four agents I'd build early. Each one has clear limits and a human check before anything sensitive reaches a customer.

01

Conversation agent

Answers common questions in the channel, qualifies intent and hands off to Sales with a summary. Never talks pricing or legal specifics on its own.

check rules + sampled human review
out routed lead + summary

02

Template builder

Drafts and localises message templates in the format each platform approves, from a master message and tone guide.

check native + Legal review
out templates ready to submit

03

Test analyst

Reads every experiment against its holdout, writes the result in plain words and suggests the next test.

check I sign off the decision
out weekly test log

04

Channel health monitor

Watches opt-outs, blocks, quality ratings and reply times daily, and flags a problem before the platform does.

check alert goes to a person
out alert + likely cause

Every time I have to fix the same agent mistake twice, the fix goes into the prompt, the rules or the checks, so it doesn't happen a third time.

First 90 days

From blank page to a live pilot.

Written from the outside, so the order will change once I'm inside.

DAYS 1–30

Learn and decide

  • Map today's lifecycle journeys, funnel data and where buyers get stuck.
  • Talk to Sales and regional teams: where do customers already use WhatsApp or WeChat?
  • Review consent, data and routing with Marketing Ops, RevOps and Legal.
  • Pick the first market, use case and provider.

DAYS 31–60

Build foundations

  • Opt-in, preferences and CRM sync live.
  • First templates approved, tone guide written.
  • Channel KPIs and a dashboard agreed with Analytics.
  • First agent in testing, internal only.

DAYS 61–90

Pilot and plan

  • WhatsApp pilot live in one market, with a holdout.
  • Weekly read with Sales; fix what slows handoffs.
  • WeChat evaluation started.
  • Channel roadmap and investment case for the next two quarters.

Every use case gets a goal, a holdout and a date to decide whether to keep it.

My own samples, based on public information. Not real Remote messages.

WhatsAppOpt-in confirmation

Hi Ana, you're all set to get updates from Remote here. We'll only message you about your account and things you asked for. Reply STOP any time.

Why: sets expectations in the first message, which protects the quality rating and opt-out rate for everything after it.

WhatsAppOnboarding · stuck step

Hi David, Priya's contract is almost ready. We just need her tax ID to run her first payroll on the 25th.

Upload nowAsk a question

Why: a real deadline, one action, and a way to get help without leaving the chat. This is where messaging beats email.

WhatsAppExpansion signal

Hi Ana, it looks like your team is looking at roles in Germany. Want a quick summary of what hiring there involves, or a call with a specialist?

Send summaryBook a callNot now

Why: triggered by a product signal, not a calendar. Two levels of commitment, and an easy way out.
