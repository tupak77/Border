// how I'd build it

In HubSpot, with AI in the loop.

HubSpot foundations

Get the plumbing right first

  • Lifecycle stages agreed with RevOps: what makes a lead, a PQL, a customer.
  • Product events synced in (signup, first build, submit, plan limits) so journeys trigger on behaviour.
  • Lists and suppressions that respect consent by region.
  • Naming and reporting so everyone can see what's live and what it drives.

Agentic workflows

From insight to journey, faster

  • Drafting: AI writes first versions by audience and stage from a message brief; I edit for accuracy and tone.
  • Docs to emails: turn new docs and changelogs into lifecycle content without starting from zero.
  • Analysis: weekly read of journey results with suggested next tests.
  • QA: automated checks for links, tokens and suppressions before every send.

// copy

Written for people who skip marketing emails.

My own samples, based on public information. Not real Expo emails; product details would be checked with Product Marketing.

EmailActivation

trigger: first EAS build failed · no retry in 24h

Your first build didn't finish. Here's the usual fix.

Most first builds fail for one of three reasons: credentials, a native dependency, or the build profile. Your log points to credentials.

eas credentials

Run that, then try again. If it still fails, reply with your build ID and a human will look.

Why: helps at the exact moment people give up. Specific, one command, a real person as backup.

EmailExpansion

trigger: 3+ store submissions · never used eas update

You've submitted 3 times this month

If some of those were small fixes, you can skip the store review for them. Push JavaScript updates straight to users with EAS Update.

Setup takes about ten minutes.

Why: starts from their own behaviour and saves them time. The feature is the answer, not the headline.

EmailUpgrade

trigger: team of 4+ · near free plan limits

Your team is growing faster than your plan

Four people are building on this project, and you're close to this month's limits. Here's exactly what changes on a paid plan, and what doesn't.

Why: honest about the trade-off. Developers upgrade when the reason is clear, not when the email is loud.
